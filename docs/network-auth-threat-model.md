# Network authentication threat model (L-13)

**Future discovery; not an approved design or a property of V1.** Today every process that reaches the listener is trusted; participant and channel labels route traffic but do not authenticate anyone. V1 has no application authentication, authorization or transport encryption. Keep the loopback default and use network listeners only on an explicitly trusted network.

## Scope and assets

SYS-034 calls for authenticating remote participants before granting access to work. Decisions, answers, message bodies, histories, participant metadata, retry identities and service availability are assets. Both the request/reply socket and the dedicated change-subscription socket cross the network boundary. A client may reconnect and use new connections at any time. The local service identity response is an operational database/process check and cannot prove the server's identity to a remote client.

Consider an unrelated network host, an on-path observer or active intermediary, a revoked or formerly authorized participant, and a client able to submit malformed or repeated frames. Also consider a malicious authenticated participant, accidental credential disclosure and a lost client response after a durable commit. A compromised service host, stolen server credentials, hostile local administrators, physical access and loss of the database are separate trust and recovery problems; document any expansion of scope before claiming protection against them. Authentication alone cannot prevent an authorized client from abusing access granted to it.

## Security outcomes to design and verify

- Authenticate the service to the client as well as the participant to the service, bind the authenticated principal to the connection and reject an unknown, invalid or revoked credential before processing protected operations or establishing a subscription. Protect credentials and application traffic in transit against observation and modification; select and review an established authenticated transport rather than inventing cryptography.
- Assign principals independently of caller-supplied routing labels. Define how a principal is provisioned and mapped to labels, which principals may act as operator or agent, and how that mapping is updated. The mapping must be applied consistently to reads, writes, retries, history, service information and subscriptions. Per-record authorization and multi-user isolation are L-14 and L-15, not consequences of authentication alone.
- Preserve the durable-acknowledgment and unknown-outcome rules across authentication failures, reconnects and credential rotation. Reject a disconnected or revoked subscriber's further access according to an explicit revocation policy. Bound authentication work and connections; do not claim protection from all denial-of-service attacks.
- Keep credentials out of command arguments, logs, retry payloads and test artifacts where practical. Specify creation, secure storage, distribution, rotation, revocation, expiration, recovery and audit behavior. Decide whether local and remote access share an identity system, and what a credential compromise implies for existing sessions and retained data.

## Candidate boundary and unresolved decisions

A reviewed mutually authenticated encrypted connection to a service with provisioned participant credentials is one possible starting point. It needs a compatible transport integration for both ordinary and subscription connections, explicit server trust, principal mapping, credential lifecycle and a secure upgrade plan for every service, CLI, terminal and portable-skill client. A network proxy could instead terminate authenticated transport, but only if the service can reliably trust the proxy's asserted principal and unauthenticated clients cannot bypass it. Neither approach is selected here; compare operational cost, compatibility, failure behavior and security review before choosing.

Decide trust enrollment and server identity, whether principals represent users, agents, devices or combinations, local privilege boundaries, credential sharing and expiry, revocation latency, auditing and recovery. Define migration and downgrade behavior: a client expecting authenticated service must fail closed instead of silently falling back to the existing unauthenticated protocol. The coexistence and network exposure of legacy V1 endpoints require an explicit decision. Do not retrofit authorization by treating a caller-provided label as proof.

## Proposed acceptance evidence

After the design is approved, exercise authorized, unknown, malformed, expired and revoked client credentials from a separate host; verify denial occurs before any protected read, write or subscription. Reject an untrusted, impersonated or incorrectly named server and tampered traffic. Exercise credential rotation and revocation with an existing subscriber and new connections according to the selected policy. Verify labels cannot be impersonated under the approved principal mapping, and cover each operation class, reconnect, lost acknowledgments, safe retries, resource exhaustion and incompatible-version downgrade. Use controlled credentials and disposable data, record exact versions and topology, and obtain independent security review. These are proposed tests, not evidence that SYS-034, L-14 or L-15 is satisfied.
